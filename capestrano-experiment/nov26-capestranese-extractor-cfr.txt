$ CONVENTIONS OF THIS FILE
$ - Fontecchio's chapter IV is organized by Latin sound and position (tonic vowels, initial consonants,
$   medial clusters ...), stating CORRESPONDENCES, not ordered rules. Each correspondence he states as
$   regular is encoded. Identifiers are F + his section label (e.g. F-Tonic-ĕ, F-Med-c, F-Yod-nj);
$   $$PG: gives printed pages.
$ - ORDER: Fontecchio says (p. 50) "Because of the lack of documents to show the existence of stages and
$   establish a chronology for them, all intermediate forms are conjectured". Ordering here therefore
$   follows (i) the few dates he gives (syncope "roughly between the second and fourth centuries", p. 87;
$   b/v "by the third century", pp. 61, 71; palatalization of velars "by the seventh century", p. 99) and
$   (ii) his Vulgar Latin intermediate forms. Where an order is forced by feeding/bleeding, "ORDER:" says so.
$ - Correspondences he calls unpredictable, sporadic, analogical, lexical or morphological, or that have no
$   majority outcome, are COMMENT-ONLY (listed at the end with pages). Where he gives a majority figure or
$   a first/main outcome, that outcome is encoded and the other noted.
$
$ GENERAL EXECUTIVE DECISIONS
$ E1. Input = Classical Latin in IPA with vowel length (ː) and stress (ˈ) marked, accusative for nouns
$     (Fontecchio's convention, p. 50). AE, OE, AU = a j, o j, a w; consonantal I, U = j, w; QU = k w;
$     X = k s; r = r.
$ E2. Fontecchio's Capestranese symbols (pp. 35-45) -> IPA: ć = t͡ʃ; z = t͡s; ẓ (z with subscript) = d͡z;
$     ʒ (his ʃ-shaped [dʒ] symbol) = d͡ʒ; ś = ʃ; ñ = ɲ; æ = æ; e (schwa) = ə; double letters = geminates
$     written as two identical segments (t t, t͡ʃ t͡ʃ ...). Final unstressed a is phonetically [ɐ] and l
$     has [ɫ]-like allophones (p. 37, 44) - allophonic, not encoded. [z] occurs only as an allophone of /s/
$     before nasals and voiced stops (p. 44) and inside d͡z.
$ E3. Vulgar Latin vowels: ẹ = e, ę = ɛ, ọ = o, ǫ = ɔ (p. 97 chart).
$ E4. DiaSim has no syllables or secondary stress. "Initial vowels" = word-initial vowels (vowel-initial
$     words, p. 59); "pretonic" = the syllable directly before the stress; "posttonic non-final" = between
$     the stress and the final vowel; plain vowel symbols are unstressed.
$ E5. Primary vs secondary (post-syncope) medial clusters, which Fontecchio distinguishes (pp. 79-90),
$     cannot be told apart once syncope has applied. The rules below follow the outcome common to both; the
$     few divergences (primary bl > bbl, primary rk > rt͡ʃ vs secondary r'k > rd͡ʒ) are noted.
