$ nov26-greek-horrocks-cfr.txt
$ DiaSim cascade: Early East Greek > Mycenaean > Classical Attic-Ionic > Late Antique Koine > Late Byzantine
$   vernacular > Standard Modern Greek
$ Source: Horrocks, Geoffrey. 2017. "45. The evolution of Greek." In Klein, Joseph & Fritz (eds.), Handbook of
$   Comparative and Historical Indo-European Linguistics, vol. 1, pp. 717-732 (printed; PDF page = printed + 13).
$ Test lexica (Horrocks's own forms): greek-horrocks-early-lexicon.txt (to Mycenaean / Classical) and
$   greek-horrocks-lexicon.txt (to Standard Modern Greek).
$
$ CONVENTIONS
$ - Identifiers: H + Horrocks's section + letter (H1a, H2.5Ai-a, ...); $$PG: = printed page(s).
$ - Order: Horrocks's own period order (sections 1 > 2 > 2.5 "by late antiquity" > 3 "end of the Byzantine
$   period" > 4). Within a period he gives no relative chronology; order is by feeding/bleeding (ORDER: lines).
$ - Rules labelled GAP-FILL or INFERRED are NOT stated as sound changes by Horrocks. They are needed to reach
$   the result systems or the transcriptions that he does give, and each one says which. Remove them to get a
$   strictly stated-only cascade.
$
$ EXECUTIVE DECISIONS
$ E1. Horrocks's chapter is mostly external history; its phonology is (a) four early isoglosses (sec. 1-2.1),
$     (b) a list of changes "by late antiquity (5th and 6th centuries CE)" (sec. 2.5 A), (c) a list for "the end
$     of the Byzantine period" (sec. 3 A), and (d) modern transcriptions. The cascade follows that structure.
$ E2. Input stage ("Early East Greek") = the archaic East Greek that Horrocks takes Mycenaean to be: labiovelars
$     kʷ ɡʷ kʷʰ intact, *ā unshifted, 3pl. -nti, *ty/*ky clusters. Changes he does not mention (compensatory
$     lengthenings, contractions, loss of w, etc.) are taken as already done in the input forms: the input is a
$     pre-Attic-Ionic form of the word, not a Proto-Greek reconstruction.
$ E3. Vowels follow Horrocks's own transcriptions: η = ɛː, ει = eː, ω = oː, ου = uː (he writes /ékhu:si/,
$     /mɛ́:tɛ:r/, /kô:s/). Diphthongs are sequences: αι = a j, αυ = a w, ευ = e w.
$ E4. Accent: the ancient accent position is marked with ˈ in the input. Horrocks's change from pitch
$     contonation to a stress accent (sec. 2.5 A ii) does not move the accent, so it is comment-only; the
$     acute/circumflex distinction is not encoded.
$ E5. Line of descent: Koine and later Greek. Where Attic differs from the Koine (Attic tt vs. Koine ss,
$     p. 722), the Koine outcome is encoded.
$ E6. Horrocks writes Modern Greek with /j/ and /ç/ before front vowels (/jiˈnekes/, /eftiˈçis/); those
$     symbols are used.
$ INPUT: Early East Greek (see E2).

$ ===================================================================================================
$ 1. MYCENAEAN (sec. 1)
$ ===================================================================================================

$ H1a Assibilation of t before i (East Greek)  $$PG: 717
$ "the characteristic innovatory assibilation of /t/ before /i/ in certain lexical items and grammatical
$ formants": 3pl. e-ko-si /ékhonsi/ vs. West Greek ἔχοντι /ékhonti/.
$ TENTATIVE: Horrocks limits it to "certain lexical items and grammatical formants" without listing them.
$ Encoded only for his one formant, word-final -nti, so that words like ὅτι are not affected.
t > s / n __ i #

~Mycenaean

$ ===================================================================================================
$ 2. ARCHAIC / CLASSICAL ATTIC-IONIC (sec. 1, 2.1)
$ ===================================================================================================

$ H1b Labiovelars > dentals before front vowels, labials elsewhere  $$PG: 718
$ "the labiovelar consonants (/kw, gw, kwh/), which later evolved into dentals (before front vowels) or
$ labials (elsewhere)"; Attic τέτταρες /téttares/ 'four' (p. 719); πῶς /pô:s/ (p. 720).
$ NOTE: front vowel = [+syl,-back,-lo] (a is +front in symbolDefs, so -lo is needed to exclude it).
$ NOTE: Aeolic labials even before front vowels (Boeotian πέτταρες) and Herodotean k- (κῶς) are dialectal.
{kʷ;ɡʷ;kʷʰ} > {t;d;tʰ} / __ [+syl,-back,-lo]
{kʷ;ɡʷ;kʷʰ} > {p;b;pʰ}

$ H2.1a *ty, *ky > ss (Koine line)  $$PG: 719, 722
$ "θάλασσα, θάλαττα ... with /ss/ in most dialects but /tt/ in Attic and Boeotian. These two geminates usually
$ represent different treatments of original groups of consonants (typically /ty/ and /ky/)"; the Koine
$ prefers "/ss/ over Attic /tt/" (p. 722). See E5.
{t j;k j} > {s s;s s}

$ H2.1b Attic-Ionic *ā > ɛ̄  $$PG: 719
$ "the characteristic shift of original /a:/, preserved in Mycenaean, to /ɛ:/, as in μήτηρ /mɛ́:tɛ:r/ 'mother'
$ < μάτηρ /má:tɛ:r/". Horrocks gives no conditioning (the Attic exception after e, i, r is not mentioned).
[+syl,+lo,+long] > [-lo,-tense]

$ GAP-FILL u > y  (not stated by Horrocks)
$ Horrocks's late-antique result is "a 6-vowel system, /a, e, i, y, o, u/", and /y/ later merges with /i/
$ (p. 728), but he never says where /y/ came from. Needed so that υ ends as /i/ (νύμφη > /ˈnimfi/,
$ γυναίκες /jiˈnekes/, ευτυχής /eftiˈçis/). Only short u is targeted: long uː is his ου (/ékhu:si/) and stays u.
[+syl,+hi,+back,-long] > [-back,+front]

~Classical Attic-Ionic

$ ===================================================================================================
$ 3. BY LATE ANTIQUITY (5th-6th c. CE)  (sec. 2.5 A)
$ ===================================================================================================

$ H2.5Ai-a Fortition of the u off-glide of diphthongs to a labial fricative  $$PG: 725
$ "fortition of the off-glide of diphthongs in /-u/ to a labial fricative (αυ /au/ and ευ /eu/ > /af, av/ and
$ /ef, ev/)". Horrocks does not state when f vs. v; his own forms show f before voiceless consonants
$ (ἔπαυσα /ˈepafsa/, ευτυχής /eftiˈçis/) and v elsewhere (ευγνώμων /evˈɣnomon/, ρεύμα /ˈrevma/, p. 731).
w > f / [+syl] __ [-son,-voi]
w > v / [+syl] __

$ H2.5Ai-b Itacism: raising of the long front mid vowels to i  $$PG: 725
$ "the successive raising of front vowels (both original and from other changes) to /i/". Short e stays e
$ (modern /e/). Targets: η ɛː and ει eː (εἶχα /ˈixa/, ἤθελα /ˈiθela/, πόλεις /ˈpolis/, pp. 728, 731).
$ ORDER: before H2.5Ai-c (length loss), which would merge ɛː/eː with e.
[+syl,-back,-lo,-hi,+long] > [+hi,+tense]

$ GAP-FILL αι > e  (not stated by Horrocks)
$ His transcriptions show it (παιδία /peˈðia/, γυναίκες /jiˈnekes/, pp. 728, 731) and his 6-vowel result has no
$ diphthongs, but he does not state the change.
{a j;ˈa j} > {e;ˈe}

$ H2.5Ai-c Loss of vowel-length distinctions  $$PG: 725
[+syl,+long] > [-long]

$ H2.5Aii Pitch contonation > stress accent  $$PG: 725 - COMMENT-ONLY (see E4; the accent position is kept).

$ H2.5Aiii-a Voiced stops > fricatives except after nasals  $$PG: 725
$ "the voiced plosives /b, d, g/ becoming fricatives (/v, ð, ɣ/) except after nasals".
{b;d;ɡ} > {v;ð;ɣ} / [-nas] __
{b;d;ɡ} > {v;ð;ɣ} / # __

$ H2.5Aiii-b Aspirates > fricatives  $$PG: 725
$ "the aspirated plosives /ph, th, kh/ starting to follow suit (> /f, θ, x/)".
{pʰ;tʰ;kʰ} > {f;θ;x}

$ H2.5Aiii-c Simplification of geminates  $$PG: 725
$ "Geminates were also simplified in pronunciation" (ὀμμάτιον /oˈmation/, θάλασσα > /ˈθalasa/).
$ NOTE: written as deletion before an identical segment, one rule per consonant.
p > ∅ / __ p
t > ∅ / __ t
k > ∅ / __ k
s > ∅ / __ s
m > ∅ / __ m
n > ∅ / __ n
l > ∅ / __ l
r > ∅ / __ r

$ H2.5Aiii-d Loss of h  $$PG: 725
h > ∅

$ H3Ai-b (begun) -ion > -in  $$PG: 728
$ "the ending -ιον /-ion/ had already started to develop to /-in/ in late antiquity". Unstressed o only.
o > ∅ / i __ n #

~Late Antique Koine

$ ===================================================================================================
$ 4. END OF THE BYZANTINE PERIOD  (sec. 3 A)
$ ===================================================================================================

$ H3Ai-a Merger of y with i  $$PG: 725, 728
$ "Merger of /i/ and /y/ was completed".
[+syl,+hi,+front,+round] > [-lab,-round]

$ H3Ai-c Synizesis of final -ia, -ea to -ja  $$PG: 728
$ "words ending in /-ia, -ea/ tended to undergo 'synizesis' to /-ja/, as in παιδιά /peðˈja/ 'children'
$ < παιδία /peˈðia/". The stress moves to the a (his example).
$ TENTATIVE: "tended to" - not every word (SMG ιστορία). Restricted to after a consonant (the glide needs one).
{ˈi a;ˈe a} > {j ˈa;j ˈa} / [+cons] __ #
{i a;e a} > {j a;j a} / [+cons] __ #

$ H3Aii-a Loss of final -n  $$PG: 725, 728
$ "Loss of final /-n/ became widespread". Late antiquity: "except in a small set of forms where this was a
$ distinctive marker (e.g. the accusative ... of the definite article)" - those morphological exceptions and
$ the learned retentions of SMG (ευγνώμων /evˈɣnomon/) are not encoded.
n > ∅ / __ #

$ H3Aii-b Loss of nasals before fricatives  $$PG: 728
$ "nasals were also lost before fricatives, as in νύφη /ˈnifi/ 'bride' < νύμφη /ˈnimfi/".
$ ORDER: after H2.5Aiii-b, which creates the fricative f.
[+nas,+cons] > ∅ / __ [-son,+cont]

$ H3Aiii Dissimilation of voiceless obstruent clusters  $$PG: 728
$ "Clusters of voiceless obstruents comprising stop + stop or fricative + fricative (other than s) were
$ dissimilated to give fricative + stop, as in ἑφτά /efˈta/ 'seven' < ἑπτά /epˈta/ ... If the second element
$ was /s/, the result is stop + /s/, as in ἔπαψα /ˈepapsa/ 'I stopped' < ἔπαυσα /ˈepafsa/."
$ NOTE: (1) stop + stop: the first becomes a fricative; (2) fricative + fricative (s excluded): the second becomes a
$ stop; (3) fricative + s: the fricative becomes a stop. Explicit segments, because no single feature change
$ maps p t k onto f θ x in symbolDefs (f is +strid, θ is +distr, x is neither).
{p;t;k} > {f;θ;x} / __ {p;t;k}
{f;θ;x} > {p;t;k} / {f;θ;x} __
{f;θ;x} > {p;t;k} / __ s

$ INFERRED Post-nasal voicing of voiceless stops  (not stated by Horrocks)
$ Required by his transcriptions: gerund -οντας /-ondas/ (p. 728), Θεού θέλοντος /θeˈu ˈθelondos/,
$ αντιθέσει /andiˈθesi/ (p. 731). Placed here because he cites /-ondas/ as already late Byzantine.
$ ORDER: after H2.5Aiii-a, so the new voiced stops stay stops.
{p;t;k} > {b;d;ɡ} / [+nas] __

$ H3Ai-d Aphaeresis of unaccented initial vowels  $$PG: 728 - COMMENT-ONLY
$ "many words lost their unaccented initial vowels ... μάτι /ˈmati/ 'eye' < ὀμμάτιον": lexically sporadic
$ ("many words"; SMG keeps ομάδα, ελπίδα), so not encoded.

~Late Byzantine vernacular

$ ===================================================================================================
$ 5. MODERN GREEK  (sec. 4: transcriptions only)
$ ===================================================================================================

$ INFERRED Palatal allophones of the velar fricatives before front vowels  (not stated by Horrocks)
$ Required by his SMG transcriptions γυναίκες /jiˈnekes/, ευτυχής /eftiˈçis/ (p. 731). Stops are not
$ palatalized in his transcriptions (the k of /jiˈnekes/ is written k).
{x;ɣ} > {ç;j} / __ [+syl,-back,-lo]

$ COMMENT-ONLY (morphological, analogical or learned; pp. 725-731)
$ - Accusative -an for consonant stems, remodelling as a-stems; -in for i-stems.
$ - Paradigm levelling (μαθαίνω for μανθάνω), 3pl. -ουν, -αν, aorist passive -(θ)ηκα.
$ - θα < θέλω να; να < ἵνα.
$ - Learned (katharevousa) phonology in SMG: έκταση /ˈektasi/ vs. demotic έχταση, ρεύμα /ˈrevma/ vs. ρέμα.
$ - Attic ττ for σσ; Herodotean κῶς for πῶς; Koine γίνομαι for γίγνομαι (lexical/dialectal choices).

$ OUTPUT: Standard Modern Greek (no stage flag needed at the end of the cascade).
