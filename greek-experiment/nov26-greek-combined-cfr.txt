$ HOW THE TWO SOURCES WERE COMBINED
$ C1. PIE > Classical Attic is Bubenik's (he treats it in detail; Horrocks gives four isoglosses). Horrocks's early
$     statements agree with Bubenik's where they overlap (labiovelars, Attic-Ionic *ā > ɛː, *ty/*ky clusters); the
$     more specific Bubenik versions are used. Horrocks's assibilation (-nti > -nsi), absent from Bubenik, is added
$     (H1a).
$ C2. Classical > c. 350 BCE: Bubenik's vowel shifts (u > y, ọ̄ > ū, ǭ > ō). These supply the /y/ and the u of ου
$     that the Horrocks-only cascade had to gap-fill.
$ C3. Koine to late antiquity: Horrocks's list, with Bubenik's datings and his two extra statements (ai > ę̄,
$     [zd] > [z]). Where they differ, the more specific statement is used: voiced stops > fricatives "except after
$     nasals" (Horrocks) rather than Bubenik's unconditioned version.
$ C4. Medieval and Modern: Horrocks only (Bubenik stops at c. 600 CE).
$ C5. Bubenik's executive decisions E1-E6 and Horrocks's E3-E6 carry over. Vowel notation is Bubenik's:
$     ẹ̄ = eː (ει), ę̄ = ɛː (η), ọ̄ = oː (ου, later uː), ǭ = ɔː (ω, later oː).
