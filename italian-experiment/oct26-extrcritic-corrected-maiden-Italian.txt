$ Source: Maiden, Martin. 1995 [Routledge reissue 2013]. A Linguistic History of Italian. Longman
$   Linguistics Library. Chapter 2, "History of the sound system" (pp. 24-91).
$   (file: "(Longman linguistics library) Maiden, Martin - A linguistic history of Italian-Routledge (2013).pdf";
$   printed page = PDF page - 19). Uses the standard gamma symbolDefs.csv (no extensions needed).
$
$ CONVENTIONS OF THIS FILE
$ - Identifiers are Maiden's section numbers (e.g. M4.2 = section 4.2). Where one section yields several
$   changes, they are labelled M5.2a, M5.2b, ...
$ - $$PG: = printed page(s) in Maiden.
$ - ORDER: Maiden's chapter is organized by TOPIC, not by date, so the rules cannot simply follow the
$   page order. They are ordered by his dating statements (e.g. yod palatalization "in, or before, the
$   second century"; velar palatalization "in about the fifth century") and by the intermediate forms in
$   his tables (e.g. the "P-R lengthening" column of (36), the Proto-Romance forms of (5), (6), (21)).
$   The basis for each placement is given under "ORDER:".
$ - Changes Maiden describes as sporadic tendencies, lexically/morphologically conditioned, analogical,
$   hypercorrect in only some words, or confined to non-standard Tuscan (gorgia, intervocalic
$   deaffrication, etc.) are COMMENT-ONLY entries, listed at the end with their pages so nothing he
$   describes is lost.
$
$ GENERAL EXECUTIVE DECISIONS
$ E1. Input = Classical Latin in IPA, with CL vowel length (ː) and CL stress (ˈ) MARKED in the lexicon.
$     Maiden describes the CL stress rule (1.2, p. 25) but it is a property of the input, not a change.
$     Latin AE, OE, AU = a j, o j, a w; consonantal I, U = j, w; X = k s; QU = k w; GU = ɡ w; r = r (trill).
$ E2. Output = standard Italian. Where Maiden distinguishes the standard from other Tuscan outcomes, the
$     standard one is encoded (e.g. intervocalic sj > t͡ʃ, M5.2).
$ E3. Geminates (Maiden's long consonants) = two identical segments (t t); a geminate affricate = two
$     identical affricates (t͡s t͡s for Maiden's [tts]).
$ E4. DiaSim has no syllables. "Open syllable" = the vowel is followed by a vowel, by #, by one
$     non-syllabic + vowel, or by an obstruent + liquid + vowel (muta cum liquida forms an onset, p. 25).
$     "Closed" = followed by two consonants not forming such an onset, or by a consonant + #.
$ E5. Plain vowel symbols are UNSTRESSED; stress-neutral changes list the grades ({e;ˈe}). Secondary
$     stress is not used.
$ E6. When a source statement has more than one reading, the reading supported by Maiden's examples is
$     encoded and the alternative noted. Where he gives two outcomes (doublets), the first-listed/main
$     one is encoded and the other noted.
$ E7. Front vowels (i e ɛ) = [+syl,+front,-lo], since DiaSim's a is also [+front].
