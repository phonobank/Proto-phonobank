$ Learned (and semi-learned) borrowings from Latin cited by Maiden (1995/2013). Kept apart from ItalLex.txt
$ because these words did not go through the regular sound changes. Page numbers are printed pages
$ (PDF page - 19). Latin follows ItalLex.txt conventions (CL length, accusative -m, ae = a e, au = a w,
$ Greek aspirates pʰ tʰ kʰ, y). Where Maiden gives a non-classical etymon, his form is used and the CL
$ form is noted. Italian = standard pronunciation; Maiden says learned words have open stressed mid
$ vowels (p. 84) and a voiced intervocalic sibilant (p. 63).
$ Left out: Greek-only words (deissi, ctonico, bdellio, mnemonico, atmosfera, eczema, rizoma, amnistia,
$ tecnica, -ologo words), the modern coinage incapsulare, suffixes (-issimo, -ario, -izzare, -colo),
$ ecclesiastical words cited for [z] (chiesa, cresima, battesimo etc., p. 63: Maiden treats these as
$ evidence of northern borrowing, not as learned), and the place name Genova (p. 71).
