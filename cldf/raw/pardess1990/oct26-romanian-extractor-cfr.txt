$ CONVENTIONS OF THIS FILE
$ - Rules appear in the order Pardess gives them (Ch. 4 = R1-R25C, Ch. 5 = R26A-R45B, Ch. 6 = R46A-R60).
$ - Each rule is headed by Pardess's own identifier (R1, R2A, ... R60) and $$PG: = page number(s) as PRINTED
$   in the dissertation (printed page = PDF page - 22).
$ - Where one Pardess rule needs several DiaSim lines, the lines are labelled R13B-i, R13B-ii, etc.
$ - "ORDER:" remarks record ordering constraints that Pardess states or that his derivations entail,
$   referring to rules by Pardess's identifiers.
$ - "NOTE:" remarks record translation decisions specific to one rule; general decisions are listed below.
$ - Rules that Pardess himself describes as morphological / lexical are kept as comment-only entries
$   (marked COMMENT-ONLY) so that their identifiers remain retrievable.
$
$ GENERAL EXECUTIVE DECISIONS (format translation)
$ D1. Pardess's transcription -> DiaSim/IPA symbols:
$       y (glide) = j ; w = w ; e-/o- with subscript arch = e̯ / o̯ ; B (beta) = β ;
$       c-hacek = t͡ʃ ; j-hacek = d͡ʒ ; ts = t͡s ; dz = d͡z ; s-hacek = ʃ ; z-hacek = ʒ ;
$       lambda = ʎ ; n-tilde = ɲ ; eng = ŋ ; schwa = ə ; barred-i = ɨ ; open e = ɛ ; open o = ɔ ;
$       g = ɡ (IPA script g, U+0261) ; length ':' = ː ;
$       w/y with under-ring (devoiced glides) = w̥ / j̥.
$     Pardess's y is ALWAYS the palatal glide /j/, never the rounded vowel /y/.
$ D2. Pardess's [r] is a flap and his [rr] a trill. DiaSim: flap = ɾ, trill = r.
$     Hence Latin single r must be ɾ in the input lexicon, and Latin -rr- = ɾ ɾ until R26A.
$     Pardess's ad hoc feature [rep] (r vs rr) does not exist in DiaSim; in DiaSim the flap/trill
$     contrast is carried by [cont] (ɾ -cont, r +cont). Rules stated with [rep] are written with symbols.
$ D3. Geminates are sequences of two identical segments (t t, l l, n n, ...), since DiaSim
$     separates phones with spaces. A geminate affricate (Pardess "cc-hacek", "tts") is two identical
$     affricate segments (t͡ʃ t͡ʃ, t͡s t͡s).
$ D4. Stress: Pardess's [+acc] (primary stress, acute accent) = DiaSim [+prim] on the vowel (ˈV);
$     [-acc] = [-stres] for vowels. In DiaSim the plain vowel symbols (a, e, i, o, u, ə ...) are the
$     UNSTRESSED ones, so a symbol rule on "e" hits only unstressed e. Where Pardess's rule is
$     stress-neutral, features or stress-variant disjunctions ({a;ˌa;ˈa}) are used instead.
$ D5. Pardess's feature names: [high] = hi, [low] = lo, [acc] = prim/stres, [voice] = voi,
$     [str] = strid, [del rel] = delrel, [voc] has no DiaSim counterpart (vowels = [+syl], glides =
$     [-syl,-cons]).
$ D6. Pardess (1990) predates [front]: his [a], [ə], [ɨ] are [+back], his [e], [i], [ɛ] [-back].
$     In DiaSim, a is [+front,-back,+lo] and ə / ɨ are [-back,-front]. So Pardess's "[-back] V"
$     (= i e ɛ) is written [+syl,+front,-lo], and his "[+back] V" set is not a single DiaSim class;
$     such rules are written with explicit symbols or split.
$ D7. Pardess treats nasals as [-cont] stops; in DiaSim nasals are [+cont]. Where he means
$     "stop, oral or nasal" this is written [αcont,αnas] (both - = oral stop, both + = nasal).
$     Where his "[+cont]" class is meant to include n (R41A), symbols are used.
$ D8. Pardess's class letters: C = [+cons]; V = [+syl]; N = [+nas,+cons]; Co = ([+cons])*
$     (any number of consonants); W = [-syl,-cons]. '#' = word boundary.
$     Pardess's $ (syllable boundary) cannot be used: DiaSim has no syllable boundaries and $ is
$     the DiaSim comment character; it is replaced case by case (see R7).
$     Pardess's '//' ("in the presence of", i.e. on either side) is split into two rules,
$     one with the context before and one with the context after the focus.
$ D9. Pardess's outputs often give only the features he considers distinctive; in DiaSim every feature
$     that must change for the output to be a defined phone is specified (e.g. k > p also needs
$     [-hi,-back]); where a feature matrix cannot reach a defined phone cleanly, symbols are used.
$ D10. Sporadic or lexically restricted rules are encoded as general rules (DiaSim cannot restrict them
$     lexically) and flagged "SPORADIC/LEXICAL" in a NOTE.

$ ORDER: Pardess orders R2A before R2B (AE monophthongized before OE, after Väänänen).

$ R3A Reduction of final [m] (P6A)  $$PG: 48-49
$ Pardess: m > n / __ # in monosyllabic words; m > ∅ / __ # in all others.
$ NOTE: "monosyllabic" = only one [+syl] in the word. Pardess's own examples conflict with the
$ monosyllable clause (kum > cu, kwam > kwa lose the m; only kwem > kwen keeps a nasal).
$ ORDER: R3A-i must precede R3A-ii.
m > n / # ([-syl])* [+syl] __ #
m > ∅ / __ #

$ R3B Reduction of [n] + fricative (P6B)  $$PG: 49-50
$ V n [-son,+cont] > V[+long] ∅ [-son,+cont]
$ NOTE: some past participles are exceptions (prensu > prins, responsu > raspuns) - LEXICAL.
[+syl] n > [+long] ∅ / __ [-son,+cont]

$ R4A Syncope (P4)  $$PG: 50-51
$ A short high vowel is deleted in an internal syllable (usually post-tonic) when preceded by a
$ single resonant and followed by a single consonant, or vice versa:
$   V[+high,-long] > ∅ / V Ci __ Cj V, where Ci and/or Cj = R (resonant).
$ NOTE: split into the two cases (R before, R after). [-stres] added: a syncopated vowel is by
$ definition unstressed, and Pardess's formula would otherwise delete stressed i/u too.
$ NOTE: SPORADIC/LEXICAL - Pardess: "not applied universally ... one must verify the DR form"
$ (lepore, maskulu are exceptions). assula > asla has a geminate before the vowel, which the
$ single-C formula does not cover.
$ ORDER: R4B applies concurrently with or immediately after R4A.
[+syl,+hi,-long,-stres] > ∅ / [+syl] [+son,+cons] __ [+cons] [+syl]
[+syl,+hi,-long,-stres] > ∅ / [+syl] [+cons] __ [+son,+cons] [+syl]

$ R4B Dissimilation of [tl]: [tl] > [kl]  $$PG: 51-52
$ Pardess's formula: t > k / __ l
$ NOTE: Pardess adds (after Väänänen) that [sl] likewise needed a 'consonant of passage' [k], before
$ which [ss] degeminated (ássla > áskla). That part comes from his prose and example, not the formula,
$ and is encoded as R4B-ii. The degemination ss > s before k then follows from R8.
$ ORDER: concurrently with or immediately after R4A (Pardess).
t > k / __ l
∅ > k / s __ l

$ R5A Deletion of word-final oral or nasal stop (P7)  $$PG: 52-53
$ Pardess: [C,-cont] > ∅ / __ #  (his [-cont] includes nasals; D7).
$ NOTE: split into oral stops/affricates and nasal consonants. [-son] is needed in the first line
$ because the DiaSim flap ɾ is also [-cont] and final r is not deleted here (cf. R5C).
$ ORDER: after R3A (final m already handled there). Pardess's kwem > kwen > kwene implies the
$ final -e of R5B was added to kwen before R5A could delete its n.
[-son,-cont] > ∅ / __ #
[+nas,+cons] > ∅ / __ #

$ R5B Addition of word-final [e] after [l]  $$PG: 53
∅ > e / l __ #

$ R5C Metathesis of word-final [r]  $$PG: 53-54
$ V r # > r V #
$ NOTE: metathesis written as insertion + deletion. Pardess notes that in some words final r was
$ simply lost (frater > frate, per > pe) - LEXICAL, not encoded.
∅ [+syl] ɾ > ɾ [+syl] ∅ / [+cons] __ #

$ R6 Fronting of [w] to [β] (P10)  $$PG: 54-55
$ Pardess: w > β (unconditioned).
$ NOTE: restricted to w before a vowel and not after a velar. Pardess's own later derivations keep
$ the labiovelars kw/gw (kwéro, sángwe ... until R11/R17) and the diphthong aw (awru >> aur);
$ all his examples of R6 have w before a vowel, initially or after a vowel, r, l or z.
$ ORDER: R6 must precede R8 (egzwolare > egzβolare > ezβolare). R6 precedes R21.
w > β / {#;[-cons];[+ant];[+cor]} __ [+syl]

$ R7 Fortition of syllable initial [y]: [y] > [dy]  $$PG: 55-56
$ Pardess: y > dy / $ __  ($ = syllable boundary).
$ NOTE: DiaSim has no syllable boundary; all of Pardess's examples are word-initial, so $ is
$ rendered as # (word-initial). Pardess adds that the fortition probably began intervocalically
$ (where yod was geminate); that case is not covered.
$ ORDER: precedes R18B, which turns dy into d͡ʒ.
∅ > d / # __ j

$ R8 Simplification of K[s]K, K[z]K clusters (P8)  $$PG: 56
[-son] > ∅ / __ [+strid] [-son]

$ R9 Deletion of [k]: [nkt] > [nt]  $$PG: 56
$ NOTE: written with [+nas,+cons] in case the input lexicon transcribes the nasal before k as ŋ.
k > ∅ / [+nas,+cons] __ t

$ R10Ca Partial assimilation of velars (P12, P13)  $$PG: 58
$ After a stressed vowel a velar stop, oral or nasal, becomes bilabial before an alveolar consonant:
$ [ks] > [ps], [kt] > [pt], [ŋn] > [mn].
$ Pardess: [-cont,-ant,-cor] > [+ant] / V[+acc] __ C[+ant,+cor]
$ NOTE: input "-cont" (oral + nasal stops, D7) = [αcont,αnas]. Output needs [+lab,-hi,-back] too,
$ so that k/ɡ/ŋ become p/b/m (D9). The following consonant is restricted to alveolar obstruents and
$ nasals ([αson,αnas] ...): the liquids l, ɾ are [+ant,+cor] as well, but kl/gl clusters
$ (oklu, veklu) clearly do not become pl (cf. R43).
$ NOTE: Pardess's example laktúka > laptúka has the velar AFTER AN UNSTRESSED vowel, which his formula
$ (and the R10Ca/R10Cb split) does not predict.
[+cons,-ant,-cor,αcont,αnas] > [+ant,+lab,-hi,-back] / [+syl,+prim] __ [+cons,+ant,+cor,βson,βnas]

$ R10Cb Total assimilation of velars  $$PG: 59-60
$ Before stress, total assimilation seems to have occurred in most cases:
$ Pardess: [-cont,-ant,-cor] > [+ant,+cor] / V[-acc] __ C[+ant,+cor]
$ Examples: laksare > lassare, eksire > essire, fraksinetu > frassinetu, koŋnoskere > konnoskere.
$ NOTE: "total assimilation" = the velar becomes identical to the following alveolar; written as one
$ line per possible following alveolar (s, t, n) so the output is a defined phone.
$ NOTE: Pardess hedges: "in most cases" (koŋnatu >> cumnat is an exception).
[+cons,-ant,-cor,αcont,αnas] > s / [+syl,-prim] __ s
[+cons,-ant,-cor,αcont,αnas] > t / [+syl,-prim] __ t
[+cons,-ant,-cor,αcont,αnas] > n / [+syl,-prim] __ n

$ R11 Labialization of [kw] and [gw] before [a]: [kwa] > [pwa], [gwa] > [bwa]  $$PG: 60-61
$ Pardess: [-son,-ant,-cor] > [+ant] / __ wa
$ NOTE: output completed with [+lab,-hi,-back] (D9).
$ NOTE: Pardess's example lingua > limbwa also shows the nasal assimilating to the new labial. That
$ follows from his example, not from his formula, so it is encoded as a separate line, R11-ii.
$ R11-ii assumes the input lexicon has velar ŋ before velars, as Pardess transcribes (líŋgua).
$ NOTE: Pardess: before R11 (anticipating R17) QUALIS, QUAM, QUANDO, QUANTUS, SQUAMA lost the wau
$ (kale, ka, kando, kantu, skama), so R11 could not apply to them - LEXICAL, not encoded.
$ ORDER: before R17 (w > ∅ / __ V).
[-son,-ant,-cor] > [+ant,+lab,-hi,-back] / __ w [+syl,+lo]
[+nas,+cons,-ant,-cor] > [+ant,+lab,-hi,-back] / __ [-son,+lab] w [+syl,+lo]

$ R12 Prothesis: initial [s]C > [is]C (P14)  $$PG: 61-62
$ ∅ > V[+high,-back] / # __ s C[-son]
$ ORDER: R14 later removes this vowel again (after R13G has lowered it to e).
∅ > i / # __ s [+cons,-son]

$ R13A Lengthening of second vowel in vowel combinations and concomitant shift in stress (P15A)  $$PG: 62-63
$ Pardess: V[-low,+acc] V > V[-low,-acc] V[+long,+acc]
$ Examples: muliere > mulié:re, pariete > parié:te, ariete > arié:te, kapreolu > kapreó:lu.
$ NOTE: the first vowel's [-low] is not repeated in the output (it does not change).
$ NOTE: as written, Pardess's formula would also hit púteu, mórtuu, kamísia, wí:nea, which in his
$ own later derivations keep their stress (pútiu, mórtu, kamísya, wí:nia). All his R13A examples have
$ a short FRONT first vowel and a MID second vowel, so the rule is restricted accordingly.
$ ORDER: R13A precedes R15 (which needs the long stressed vowel R13A creates).
[+syl,-lo,-back,-long,+prim] [+syl,-hi,-lo] > [-stres,-prim] [+long,+stres,+prim]

$ R13B Raising of [o] and [e] (P15B)  $$PG: 63
$ Short, unstressed [o] and [e] become [u] and [i] when adjacent to another vowel.
$ Pardess: V[-low,-long,-acc] > [+high] // V
$ NOTE: '//' = on either side, hence two lines. The plain symbols e, o are the unstressed short ones (D4).
{e;o} > {i;u} / __ [+syl]
{e;o} > {i;u} / [+syl] __

$ R13C Contraction of vowel combinations (P18B)  $$PG: 63
$ Two sequential identical vowels coalesce: Vi Vi > Vi  (mórtuu > mórtu)
$ NOTE: identity is expressed with a separate alpha variable for each feature; the second vowel is deleted.
[+syl,αhi,βlo,γback,δfront,εround,ζlong] > ∅ / [+syl,αhi,βlo,γback,δfront,εround,ζlong] __

$ R13D Loss of syllabicity of high vowels (P15)  $$PG: 64
$ The short, unstressed high vowels [i] and [u] become the glides [y] and [w] when adjacent to another vowel.
$ Pardess: V[+high,-long,-acc] > [-syl] // V
$ NOTE: Pardess: "This rule seems to be recurrent", i.e. it applies from this point on whenever
$ the environment arises. DiaSim applies it only here.
{i;u} > {j;w} / __ [+syl]
{i;u} > {j;w} / [+syl] __

$ R13E Laxing of stressed [e] to [ɛ] (P15Da)  $$PG: 64-65
$ Pardess: V[-long,-high,-back,+acc] > [-tense]
$ NOTE: Pardess's [-back] includes a in DiaSim (D6), hence [+front,-lo].
[+syl,-long,-hi,-lo,+front,+prim] > [-tense]

$ R13F Lengthening of word-final [-i(s)]  $$PG: 65
$ Pardess: V[+high,-back] > [+long] / __ (C) #   (fakitis > fakiti:s)
$ ORDER: R13F bleeds R13G (prevents 2pl -tis > -te). Pardess notes the alternative that R13G was

[+syl,+hi,+front] > [+long] / __ ([+cons]) #

$ R13G Lowering of [i]: stressed or unstressed [i] > [e] (P15Db)  $$PG: 66-67

$ R14 Apheresis: loss of unstressed initial [e] before [s] or [z] before a consonant  $$PG: 67

$ Pardess: Ci Cj > Cj Cj where Cj = [-cont,-ant,+cor,+del rel]  (pútt͡ʃu > pút͡ʃt͡ʃu)

$ R19 Depalatalization: [d͡ʒ], [t͡ʃ] > [d͡z], [t͡s] after a stressed vowel (P32)  $$PG: 73
$ Pardess: [-ant,+cor,+del rel] > [+ant] / V[+acc] __
$ NOTE: output completed with [-distr,-front] so that t͡ʃ/d͡ʒ reach t͡s/d͡z. Pardess's example

$ R23 Degemination of consonants except the alveolar resonants [ll], [nn] and [rr] (P28)  $$PG: 78-79
$ Pardess: Ci Ci > Ci where Ci is not [+ant,+cor,+son,+cons]
$ NOTE: identity of the two consonants is expressed with alpha variables (one per feature); line
$ (i) covers obstruents, line (ii) the non-coronal sonorant consonants (e.g. m m). l l, n n, ɾ ɾ are
$ [+cor] sonorants and are excluded, as Pardess requires.
