================================================================================
AUDIT REPORT: Faithfulness of "july2026-extractor-rules-Latin-Romanian" to
Pardess (1990), "From Latin to Romanian: Phonological Rules" (UCLA diss.),
and to the DiaSim cascade format (https://github.com/clmarr/DiaSim/wiki/Cascade,
symbolDefs.csv gamma branch)
================================================================================

METHOD
------
Every rule (R1-R60 and all lettered sub-rules) was checked against the
actual dissertation page(s) cited in the extraction's own "$$PG:" flags,
against the DiaSim cascade format specification (fetched fresh from the
GitHub wiki), and against the exact feature vectors in symbolDefs.csv
(gamma branch, fetched fresh from GitHub). The dissertation's front-matter
(pp. vii-xii) supplied Pardess's own feature/segment/abbreviation key,
needed because his SPE notation does not map 1:1 onto symbolDefs.csv. The
work was split across five parallel review passes (Ch.4 R1-R14, Ch.4
R15-R25C, Ch.5 R26A-R35B, Ch.5 R36A-R45B, Ch.6 R46A-R60 + Ch.7 + Ch.8
synoptic index) plus the author's own direct read of R1-R5C, and cross-
checked; the corrected cascade file
("july26-extrcritic-corrected-pardess-Latin-to-Romanian.txt") implements
every finding below except where noted as "not corrected" (with reason).

Overall picture: the extraction gets the big structural decisions right
(the geminate-as-sequence convention, the black-stage flagging, the
identification of non-phonological rules as comment-only, the general
shape of most rules) and several of its own flagged "Executive Decisions"
turned out to be accurate on inspection (R13F/R13G labeling, R52A/R52B
labeling). But a substantial number of rules contain real, independently
confirmable errors: reversed environments, mis-transcribed vowels/
consonants, features that don't pick out the phones intended, structural
under-specification that breaks the rule's own stated identity
requirements, and at least one clear rule-ordering bug. Two of the "source
is OCR-damaged, reconstruction is tentative" claims (R15, R46A/R46B) are
themselves false -- both pages are legible and a much fuller, non-tentative
reconstruction was possible. One claim within a rule's own commentary
(at R52B, "no separate R57 exists elsewhere") is self-contradicted by the
cascade file's own R57 rule two pages later.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 0: EXECUTIVE DECISIONS (the file's own front-matter claims)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ED1 (rhotic mapping, tap ɾ vs. trill r): The tap/trill reinterpretation
itself is well-motivated and matches Pardess's story, BUT the claim that
ɾ is used "throughout" the cascade is false: R5C and R10A (both earlier
than R26A/B, where the split is actually introduced) still use plain "r",
undifferentiated. This is correct as far as it goes (the split genuinely
shouldn't apply before R26A), but the header's own wording overclaims.
Also, Pardess frames the tap-underlies-CL-rr analysis as an EXPLICIT,
HEDGED ASSUMPTION ("We are assuming here that -RR- in Classical Latin was
somehow a doubled version of a single flap [r]. In the event that -RR-
was already a multiple vibrant in CL, this rule would not be necessary,"
p.86 fn.) -- the extraction presents it as settled. And the gloss
"(repeated)" for Pardess's ad hoc [+rep] feature is this extraction's own
INFERENCE, not a quotation; Pardess never defines [rep] in the R26A/B
prose, and his own front-matter abbreviations list glosses it oddly as
"repressiveness" (apparently an OCR/typo artifact for "repetitiveness").
CORRECTED: header and R26A/R26B notes reworded to state the split begins
at R26A, to flag the assumption as hedged, and to mark "(repeated)" as
inferred.

ED4 (word-length/morpheme conditioning): accurate as a general policy,
but misapplied at R30 (see below) -- R30's restriction is a closed lexical
set, not word-length or article-morpheme conditioning. CORRECTED: ED4 tag
removed from R30's comment.

ED6 (claimed OCR damage at R15 and R46A/R46B): FALSE for both rules. See
R15 and R46A/R46B sections below -- both pages are fully legible (verified
by direct inspection AND an independent pdftotext extraction) and were
reconstructed in full, not left as tentative fragments.

ED8 (R13F/R13G Ch.8 mislabeling claim): CONFIRMED ACCURATE. The Ch.8
synoptic index (p.138) does read "R13F i > e" with no separate synoptic
entry for the word-final -i(s) lengthening rule that the body text calls
R13F. No correction needed.

ED9 (analogical rules retained as comment-only): sound policy, correctly
applied at R25C, R60. No correction needed.

R4A -- ERROR (p.50-51). Pardess's environment is "V Ci __ Cj V" -- the
target vowel must be flanked on the OUTSIDE by vowels as well as by the
inner consonants (syllable-internal syncope), not merely bounded by
consonants with no outer-vowel requirement. The extraction's two lines
omitted the outer flanking vowels, over-generating to word edges and
consonant clusters with no vowel present. FIXED: both lines now require
[+syl] on both outer edges.

fortition "probably began in intervocalic position in which it had
always been geminated (Väänänen 1974:115)." FIXED: note corrected to
"intervocalic, gemination" rather than "post-consonantal".

R8 -- ERROR, confirmed serious (p.56). Left/right context reversed.
Pardess's printed rule has NO left context, with strident+obstruent on
the RIGHT: "[-son] > ∅ / ___ [+strid] [-son]". The extraction instead put
the strident on the LEFT. Checked against all three of Pardess's own

extraction's version as written matches none of them. FIXED: environment
corrected to "__ [+strid] [-son]".

R9, R10A, R10B -- OK, no issues (p.56-57).

R10Ca -- ERROR (p.58). (1) Target class "[-cont,-ant,-cor]" wrongly

his own examples (dígnu>dímnu, lígnu>límnu, púgnu>púmnu, sígnu>símnu) are
all nasal cases. (2) The "αnas" agreement was tied to the following
alveolar (implying assimilation of nasality to the following consonant);
Pardess's rule instead simply carries the target's OWN nasality through
unchanged. FIXED: "-cont" dropped from target; αnas re-tied to the
target's own input/output only.

R10Cb -- ERROR, multiple (p.59-60). (1) Same target-class bug as R10Ca.
(2) The rule's title cited "[rjn]>[nn]" -- there is no such sequence on

(3) The claim that "pretonic" position "cannot be directly encoded" and
needs an invented right-scanning lookahead is false: Pardess's printed
rule states the condition as a plain LEFT context ("the immediately

Missing exception: koŋnátu > DR cumnat shows the PARTIAL (R10Ca-style)

Pardess states total assimilation occurred "in most cases," not
universally. (5) Misattributed citation: Pardess credits Vasiliu with
the ADOPTED analysis ("with R10C we posit, with Vasiliu, that Latin -GN-
became Romanian [mn]..."), not with the competing, dispreferred
alternative (via an intermediate [ɲ] stage), which the extraction's note
had backwards. FIXED: target class corrected, rule restated using
Pardess's actual left-context condition, title corrected, both notes
corrected/added.

R11 -- Minor fidelity fix (p.60-61). Target class "[-cont,-ant,-cor]"

R22D -- MAJOR ERROR, two independent problems (p.76-78). (1)
Directionality: the rule and title asserted a following [u] trigger only
("β > ɡ / __ u"), but Pardess's FIRST example, úßer > úger, has the [u]
PRECEDING the target, not following -- of five examples, only úßer needs
a left trigger, the rest have a following u. Pardess's own "//" notation
(rather than a directional "/") likely reflects exactly this
bidirectionality. FIXED: both directions given as separate lines. (2)
Rule-ordering bug: the extraction argued "R22D must precede R24... else
the derived [g] here would itself have been subject to R24's
palatalization" -- this reasoning is backwards. If R22D runs first, the
resulting "úger" has a freshly-created [g] sitting directly before a
front vowel [e], which is EXACTLY R24's target+environment -- so R24
(still to come) WOULD wrongly affricate it, giving *újer instead of the
correct "uger". The correct sequencing is the reverse: R24 must run
FIRST (while the segment is still β, a labial, invisible to R24's velar-
only target), and only then does R22D convert β>g, safely past R24's one
linear pass. As the file was originally ordered (R22D before R23 before
R24, matching Pardess's own page-presentation order), this bug would
misfire in an actual DiaSim run. FIXED: R24 physically moved to appear
before R22D in the cascade (both rules' commentary corrected to match);
see the corrected file for the full derivation-based justification. This
is the single most structurally invasive correction in this audit --
everywhere else, only individual rule lines/comments were touched, but
here getting the right OUTPUT for a worked example (úber>uger) requires
reordering two rules relative to each other.

degeminated under the previous formalization, contrary to Pardess's
stated rule (which covers all consonants except ll/nn/rr). FIXED: full

a third line added to cover m/mm.

CROSS-CUTTING ISSUE affecting R46A, R46B, R47A, R49, R50, R58: Pardess's
own theoretical vocabulary derives his schwa-like "[ə]" and high-central
"[ɨ]" via a BACKING feature change ([+back]) on front vowels, and titles
several later rules explicitly by that segment's name ("Raising of [ə]
before...", p.113-115). But symbolDefs.csv codes BOTH ə and ɨ as
[back:-] (they are central, not back, in this feature system) -- so a
literal feature-matrix rule that only flips a front vowel's [back]
feature can never resolve to DiaSim's actual "ə"/"ɨ" symbols; it produces
a feature bundle matching no real segment in the table. This was never
disclosed anywhere in the extraction's Executive Decisions (contrast with
how thoroughly the rhotic tap/trill reinterpretation, a comparable
translation problem, WAS disclosed as ED1). It also produced a visible
symptom: the extraction used three DIFFERENT placeholder names across
these rules for what should be one consistent segment -- an invented
symbol "ɔ" (at R46B), an ad hoc bracketed "[Q]" (in commentary at
R47A/R49), and the literal correct name "ə" (elsewhere) -- itself an
internal inconsistency. FIXED, throughout: every affected rule now uses
direct literal-symbol substitution (e>ə, ə>ɨ, etc.) rather than a
feature-changing rule, so it actually resolves to the segment Pardess
and the cascade's own later rules consistently refer to by name. This is
disclosed as a general caveat at the top of the corrected file as well as
individually at each affected rule.

R46A -- MAJOR ERROR (p.112-113). (1) The claim that Pardess's formal
feature-matrix statement is "visibly OCR-damaged" is FALSE -- confirmed
legible by direct inspection and an independent pdftotext pass:
"[-cons,-back,-high] > [+back] / [C,+ant,-cor] ___ Co V[+back]". This
restricts the target to NON-HIGH front vowels; the extraction's
"tentative" reconstruction wrongly admitted high [i] too (no "-high" in
its target). (2) The labial trigger must be a SINGLE, immediately
adjacent consonant (no Kleene-star before the target); the extraction
wrongly inserted one there. The Kleene-star belongs only AFTER the
target, before the eventual back vowel -- confirmed by the Ch.8
derivation table for CAPITANEUM (kapeténu>kəpaténu, where "p" sits
directly next to the target "e", and the Co gap, "tn", falls between the
target and the eventual "u"). (3) The environment's labial-consonant
matrix was missing "+cons", which (per symbolDefs.csv) would also admit
rounded back vowels (u, o, ɔ all share [+lab,-cor,-cons]) as spurious
triggers. (4) See cross-cutting issue above re: the ə/[+back] mismatch.
Word-list transcriptions (méru/péru/védu>máru/páru/vádu; exceptions méri,
péri, vézi, veáde, vecínu, mease, peanne, beare) were independently
re-verified as correct. FIXED: full reconstruction given, using direct
substitution (e>ə) with the corrected environment (single adjacent
labial before, Kleene-star + eventual back vowel after).

R46B -- MAJOR ERROR (p.113). The extraction gave this as a feature/
identity change on an invented symbol "ɔ" ("ɔ > a / __ a"). Pardess's
actual formal statement is an outright DELETION, "ə > ∅ / __ a" (title:
"Loss of [ə] before [a]"), confirmed decisively by the Ch.8 derivation
table for PINNAM (a full syllable is lost, not merely changed in
quality) and by the synoptic index's own shorthand "R46B əa > a". Neither
"ɔ" nor a mutation-to-"a" rule type is supported anywhere in the source.
FIXED: ə > ∅ / __ a.

R47A -- ERROR (p.113-114). Pardess's rule is a genuine disjunction (bare
"n", requiring nothing further, OR "m"+one consonant+a vowel); the
extraction's single formula wrongly required a following vowel in EVERY
case. It also used an invalid "?" quantifier (`([+cons])?`), not part of
DiaSim's documented optionality syntax (only "(...)", "(...)*", "(...)+"
are supported) -- the only such occurrence anywhere in the file. FIXED:
split into two lines (one per disjunct), using direct substitution
(ə>ɨ) per the cross-cutting issue above; malformed "?" removed.
