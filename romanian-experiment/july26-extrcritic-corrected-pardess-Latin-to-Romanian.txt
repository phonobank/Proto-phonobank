$ Rules are given IN THE ORDER Pardess presents them (Chapters 4-6), which
$ is his claimed diachronic ordering from Classical Latin -> Balkan Romance
$ (BR) -> Common Romanian (CR) -> Daco-Romanian (DR, = Modern Standard
$ Romanian). Page numbers of the ORIGINAL DISSERTATION (not PDF page
$ numbers) are flagged with "$$PG:". Pardess's own rule identifiers
$ (R1, R2A, R2B, ... R60) are preserved and used as the unique retrieval
$ keys for ordering remarks, both his own and any editorial ones added here.
$ Feature names follow the header row of symbolDefs.csv (gamma branch):
$ syl,son,cons,cont,delrel,lat,nas,strid,voi,sg,cg,ant,cor,distr,lab,hi,lo,
$ back,front,round,tense,rtr,long,splng,stres,prim -- these correspond
$ closely, though not perfectly, to Pardess's own SPE-style feature
$ notation, which is why re-derivation from his prose was needed rather
$ than a mechanical one-to-one symbol swap.
$
$FIXED: One systemic translation problem surfaced by the audit and NOT
$ fully resolved rule-by-rule below deserves flagging up front: Pardess's
$ own theoretical vocabulary treats the schwa-like vowel that arises in
$ Chapter 6 (his "[ə]") and the high central vowel "[ɨ]" as derived via a
$ BACKING feature change from front vowels. But DiaSim's symbolDefs.csv
$ codes both ə and ɨ as [back:-] (they are phonologically CENTRAL, not
$ back, in this feature system), while [front] is also "-" for schwa. A
$ literal feature-matrix rule that only flips [-back]->[+back] on a front
$ vowel (as several of the rules below originally did) therefore cannot
$ resolve to DiaSim's actual "ə"/"ɨ" symbols -- it produces a feature
$ bundle matching no real segment in the table. Wherever this arose
$ (R46A, R47A, R49, R50), the correction below switches to direct
$ literal-symbol substitution (e.g. "e > ə", "i > ɨ") rather than a
$ feature-changing rule, to guarantee the rule actually resolves to the
$ segment Pardess and the cascade's own later rules (R47A, R49, R58)
$ subsequently refer to by name. See the report for full discussion.

$ ============================================================================
$ EXECUTIVE DECISIONS (format-translation matters worth flagging explicitly)
$ ============================================================================
$
$ (1) RHOTIC MAPPING. Pardess formalizes CL geminate -rr- becoming a
$   "true trill" and later simplifying using an ad hoc feature [+rep]/
$   [-rep] ("repeated") that does not exist in symbolDefs.csv. Since
$   symbolDefs.csv independently distinguishes a tap /ɾ/ ([-cont]) from a
$   trill /r/ ([+cont]), and this happens to align with Pardess's own
$   story (single intervocalic CL r = tap; geminate/initial-strengthened
$   r = trill; the trill later reduces back to a tap in Daco-Romanian,
$   matching modern Romanian's tapped rhotic), rr/trill is mapped onto
$   DiaSim symbol "r" and plain/tapped CL r onto DiaSim symbol "ɾ"
$FIXED: -- but ONLY FROM R26A/R26B ONWARD, not "throughout" the cascade as
$   originally claimed here. Rules R5C and R10A, which precede R26A/B and
$   still involve plain CL "r", correctly use bare "r" (undifferentiated,
$   pre-split) and are UNCHANGED below; only R26A onward introduces and
$   then uses the ɾ/r distinction. This is a substantive reinterpretation,
$   not a notational nicety, and is flagged at each rule (R26A, R26B, R50,
$   R51) it touches.
$FIXED: Also, Pardess himself presents the tap-underlying-CL-rr analysis
$   as an explicit ASSUMPTION, not settled fact: "We are assuming here
$   that -RR- in Classical Latin was somehow a doubled version of a
$   single flap [r]. In the event that -RR- was already a multiple
$   vibrant in CL, this rule would not be necessary" (p.86, fn.). And the
$   gloss "(repeated)" for his ad hoc [+rep]/[-rep] feature is an
$   INFERENCE from the rule titles/effect, not a quotation -- Pardess
$   never defines [rep] in prose on pp.86-87, and his own front-matter
$   abbreviations list glosses it oddly as "repressiveness" (apparently a
$   typo/OCR artifact for "repetitiveness"). See R26A/R26B below.
$
$ (2) GEMINATES AS SEGMENT SEQUENCES. Except for the rhotic (see (1)),
$   all Latin geminate consonants (nn, ll, ss, pp, etc.) are represented
$   as two adjacent identical segments (e.g. "n n"), not as a single
$   segment with a [+long] feature. This matches Pardess's own C_iC_j
$   notation for geminates/clusters in rules such as R18A, R23, R35B,
$   R48, and is how DiaSim's own example cascades treat gemination.
$FIXED: Where a rule of this shape used an UNLINKED natural-class feature
$   matrix repeated twice (e.g. "[-son] [-son] > [-son] ∅") rather than
$   alpha-linking every contrastive feature between the two positions,
$   the two positions do NOT actually enforce segment identity -- they
$   only enforce common class membership, so e.g. a hypothetical
$   surviving /pt/ or /kt/ cluster would wrongly "degeminate" too. This
$   bug recurred at R18A and R23 and is fixed at both (see below and the
$   report).
$
$ (3) SYLLABLE BOUNDARY. Pardess's formalism uses "$" to mark a syllable
$   boundary (e.g. in R7, y > dy / $__). DiaSim reserves "$" exclusively
$   for comments and has no syllable-boundary primitive (only "#" word
$   boundary and "+" morpheme boundary). Where this matters (R7), the
$   concrete examples given are all word-initial, so "$" is approximated
$   with "#", and the loss of generality is flagged in that rule's remark.
$FIXED: R53B has the identical issue (Pardess's formal statement there is
$   also "$"-based: "ea > e / __ $ Co e") but this was not flagged at
$   R53B originally, unlike at R7. Flagged now for consistency; the
$   approximation used (a following vowel reachable through zero or more
$   consonants) is retained as harmless in practice.
$
$ (4) WORD-SHAPE / MORPHOLOGICAL CONDITIONING. Several of Pardess's rules
$   are conditioned on word length (monosyllabic vs. polysyllabic: R3A,
$   R25A/B) or apply only within a specific morpheme (the definite
$   article: R29A, R29B, R36B, R60). DiaSim's phonological rule format has
$   no primitive for word-length or morpheme-specific conditioning beyond
$   the "+" morpheme boundary (whose diachronic legitimacy is itself
$   flagged as contentious in DiaSim's own documentation). These rules are
$   included with the closest phonological approximation possible, and
$   the un-encodable lexical/morphological restriction is noted as a
$   comment immediately above the rule line(s) in each case.
$FIXED: R30's restriction (a closed, arbitrary LEXICAL set: ESTE, ELLU,
$   ELLA, imperfect of "a fi", etc.) is NOT a word-length or def.-article
$   case and was mistagged as "Executive Decision 4" below; corrected to
$   describe it plainly as lexical restriction, not word-shape/morpheme
$   conditioning.
$
$ (5) NATURAL-CLASS "REPAIR CHAINS" GIVEN AS COMMENTARY, NOT RULES. In a
$   few places (notably R25A) Pardess offers a plausible phonetically
$   gradual pathway (s > h > ç > y) purely as motivation for naturalness,
$   while the actual INPUT/OUTPUT sound change he is proposing is the
$   single mapping s > y. Only the single net rule is formalized; the
$   intermediate steps are preserved as commentary, per Pardess's own
$   framing of them as motivation rather than as independently-ordered
$   sound changes.
$
$ (6) R15 (vowel contraction, p.68) and R46A/R46B (labial-conditioned
$   vowel backing, p.112-113) were originally claimed by this extraction
$   to be given by Pardess in feature notation "visibly corrupted in the
$   source scan" and only reconstructable as tentative best-guesses.
$FIXED: THIS CLAIM WAS FALSE FOR BOTH RULES. On close re-inspection
$   (including an independent pdftotext pass), Pardess's formal statements
$   at both p.68 and pp.112-113 are fully legible generative-phonology
$   rules, not damaged diagrams. Both have been reconstructed in full
$   below (R15 as a general rule rather than three isolated examples;
$   R46A/R46B corrected for a missing [-high] restriction, a wrongly
$   placed Kleene-star, a missing [+cons] restriction, and -- for R46B --
$   a wrong input symbol and wrong rule type entirely). See the report.
$
$ (7) STAGE FLAGS. "=Balkan Romance" and "=Common Romanian" are inserted
$   as BLACK stages (not gold), because Pardess explicitly states BR and
$   CR are unattested, comparatively-reconstructed stages (no texts
$   survive), so no gold comparanda exist for a lexicon column. Only
$   Classical Latin (input) and Modern Daco-Romanian (output) are
$   attested. A user with an appropriately populated lexicon could
$   reflag these "~" if intermediate gold comparanda become available.
$
$ (8) DISCREPANCY BETWEEN CHAPTER 4 AND CHAPTER 8. Pardess's own
$   "Synoptic Index" (Ch. 8.1, pp.137-141) does not always match his
$   Chapter 4-6 rule labels one-for-one -- most notably, the rule "i > e"
$   (lowering) is labeled R13G in the body text (p.65-66, where it is
$   formally stated) but is referred to as "R13F" both in the Ch.8
$   synoptic list and in at least one derivation table in Chapter 5
$   (p.97). The body-text numbering (R13G) is used as authoritative here
$   since that is where the rule is actually formalized, and the
$   discrepancy is flagged at R13F/R13G below so both citation forms are
$   retrievable. [Independently re-verified during this audit: the Ch.8
$   synoptic index at p.138 does read "R13F i > e" with no separate entry
$   at all for the word-final-lengthening rule -- confirmed accurate.]
$
$ (9) R25C (generalization of word-final -i as a grammatical/analogical
$   marker) and R60 (loss of def.-article final -l, attributed by
$   Pardess to morphological reanalysis) are, by Pardess's own
$   description, analogical/morphological changes rather than regular
$   sound changes. They are retained as comment-only entries (no rule
$   line) so the full numbered inventory remains intact and traceable,
$   consistent with Pardess situating them among his numbered rules
$   despite their non-phonological character.
$

$ORDER: R2A must precede R2B (Vaananen 1974:95): AE monophthongized before OE.
$FIXED: Pardess's formal statement (p.47) is "ay > ɛ:" (the LAX long vowel),

$ unique in the CL scheme in that it was the only lax vowel and the only
$ long vowel that did not have a short counterpart" -- i.e. AE and OE
$ (R2B) do NOT merge at this point, contra the previous mis-transcription,

ay > ɛː

$$PG:47-48 R2B -- Monophthongization of OE [oy] > [e:]
$NOTE: Pardess also reports a sporadic, lexically-restricted partial change

$$PG:48-49 R3A -- Reduction of final [m] (Otero P6A)
$NOTE: restricted to word-final position; the CL-monosyllable-only vs.

$ only the general (polysyllabic) deletion is given as an active rule line,
$ since it is the productive/default outcome.
$ [monosyllables only, not general:]  m > n / __ #
m > ∅ / __ #

$$PG:49-50 R3B -- Reduction of [n] before a fricative, w/ compensatory lengthening (Otero P6B)
$NOTE: past-participle forms (e.g. PRENSU, RESPONSU) are lexical exceptions.
[+syl] n > [+long] ∅ / __ [-son,+cont]

$$PG:50-51 R4A -- Syncope of a high vowel flanked by (at least) one resonant (Otero P4)
$NOTE: highly sporadic/lexically variable; many exceptions given (LEPORE,
$ MASCULU, etc.); eastern Romance is comparatively conservative relative to
$ syncopes that post-date this rule.
$FIXED: Pardess's formal environment is "V Ci __ Cj V" -- the target vowel
$ must ALSO be flanked on the OUTSIDE by vowels (i.e. syncope happens
$ syllable-internally, V-Ci-V(hi)-Cj-V, not merely "consonant, target,
$ consonant" with no outer vowel requirement). The original two lines
$ omitted the outer flanking [+syl] on each side, over-generating to any
$ adjacent-consonant environment including word edges/clusters. Both lines
$ below add the required outer vowel.
[+syl] [+son,+cons] __ [+cons] [+syl]
[+syl,+hi,-long] > ∅ / [+syl] [+son,+cons] __ [+cons] [+syl]
[+syl,+hi,-long] > ∅ / [+syl] [+cons] __ [+son,+cons] [+syl]

$ORDER: must apply concurrently with, or immediately after, R4A (syncope

$$PG:53 R5B -- Paragogic [e] added after word-final [l]

$$PG:54-55 R6 -- Fronting of [w] to bilabial fricative [B] (Otero P10)

$$PG:55-56 R7 -- Fortition of syllable-initial [y]: [y] > [dy]
$NOTE (Executive Decision 3): Pardess's formal statement is "y > dy / $__"

$ concrete examples given (YURARE, YOCARE, IOVIS) are word-initial, so the
$ rule is approximated with "#__"; broader syllable-initial triggering
$ environments described in the prose are not captured by this
$ approximation.
$FIXED: the prior version of this note speculated the uncaptured broader
$ environment might be "e.g. post-consonantal" -- Pardess does not say

$ position in which it had always been geminated (Väänänen 1974:115)" --
$ i.e. the missed generalization is INTERVOCALIC gemination, not a
$ post-consonantal context.
∅ > d / # __ j

$$PG:56 R8 -- Simplification of K[s/z]K cluster: obstruent deletes between strident and obstruent (Otero P8)
$ORDER: requires R6 (P10) to have already applied, to catch cases like
$ EGZBOLA:RE > EZBOLA:RE.
$FIXED: the left/right context was reversed. Pardess's formal rule (p.56)
$ has NO left context and puts the strident+obstruent sequence on the
$ RIGHT: "[-son] > ∅ / ___ [+strid] [-son]". Checked against all three of
$ his examples (abskóndere>askóndere, egzßolá:re>ezßolá:re, ekspó:nere>
$ espó:nere), the deleted obstruent always PRECEDES the strident -- the
$ previous version (strident on the left) matches none of them.
[-son] > ∅ / __ [+strid] [-son]

$$PG:56-57 R9 -- Deletion of [k] in [nkt] > [nt]
$NOTE: Otero's P12 ([kt]>[xt]) explicitly does NOT apply in Balkan Romance

k > ∅ / n __ t

$$PG:57 R10A -- Sporadic assimilation of [r] to [s]: [rs]>[ss] (Otero P13A)
$NOTE: sporadic -- URSU, PERSIKA are unaffected exceptions.
$NOTE (Executive Decision 1): precedes the R26A/B tap/trill split; "r"
$ here is CL's single undifferentiated rhotic.
r > s / __ s

$$PG:57 R10B -- Assimilation of [p] to [s]: [ps]>[ss] (Otero P13)
$NOTE: Romanian conserves CL [pt] generally (SEPTE, SEPTIMANA unaffected);

p > s / __ s

$$PG:58-59 R10Ca -- Partial (place-only) assimilation of velars to a following alveolar, after a stressed vowel: [ks]>[ps], [kt]>[pt], [gn]>[mn] (Otero P12, P13)
$FIXED: the target class "[-cont,-ant,-cor]" wrongly excludes the velar
$ NASAL [ŋ] (which symbolDefs.csv marks [+cont], unlike oral k/g), even
$ though Pardess's own rule explicitly covers "a velar stop, ORAL OR
$ NASAL" and his own worked examples (dígnu>dímnu, lígnu>límnu, púgnu>
$ púmnu, sígnu>símnu) are all nasal cases. "-cont" is dropped from the
$ target below so ŋ is included.
$FIXED: the "αnas" agreement was previously tied to the RIGHT CONTEXT
$ (the following alveolar), implying the rule assimilates the target's
$ nasality to what follows. Pardess's rule does not do this: the target's
$ OWN nasality (oral k/g stay oral; nasal ŋ stays nasal) simply carries
$ through unchanged. αnas below links the target's own input and output,
$ not the right context.
[-ant,-cor,αnas] > [+ant,+lab,-cor,αnas] / [+syl,+stres] __ [+ant,+cor]

$$PG:59-60 R10Cb -- Total (place+manner) assimilation of velars to a following alveolar, in PRETONIC position: [ks]>[ss], [ŋn]>[nn]
$FIXED (title): the previous title read "[rjn]>[nn]" -- there is no

$$PG:64 R13D -- Loss of syllabicity of unstressed high vowels adjacent to another vowel: [i],[u] > [y],[w] (Otero P15)
$NOTE: Pardess states this rule is RECURRENT, i.e. it continues to re-apply

$$PG:68 R15 -- Contraction of vowels in hiatus of similar height to a single long vowel (Otero P18Aab)
$FIXED (Executive Decision 6): the previous claim that Pardess's general
$ feature formula here is "visibly corrupted" and unreconstructable was
$ mistaken. p.68 is legible (both on direct inspection and an independent
$ pdftotext pass) and states a complete, general rule: a short vowel
$ deletes after a consonant when followed by a long, stressed vowel of
$ matching backness -- "[V,-long,αback] > ∅ / C __ [V,+long,+acc,αback]".
$ The general rule is given below as the active line; Pardess's three
$ worked examples are retained as an illustrative comment only.
$FIXED: the "o o" example was also transcribed backwards. Pardess's
$ example is koórte > kó:rte (FIRST o short/unstressed, SECOND o long/
$ stressed -- the survivor), matching the general rule and the other two
$ examples (preé:ndere>pré:ndere; kwié:tu>kwé:tu), where the first,
$ short vowel deletes and the second, long one survives. The previous
$ version instead had the first o surviving/lengthening and the second
$ deleting -- backwards, and inconsistent with its own sibling lines.
$ Examples (illustrative only, all now consistent: first/short vowel
