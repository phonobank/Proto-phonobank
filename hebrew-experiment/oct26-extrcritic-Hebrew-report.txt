--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. DIASIM FORMAT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A1. 3.2 (first apocope) and 3.5 (second apocope): "{a;e} > ∅ / __ #".
    A two-member disjunction cannot map onto a bare ∅. DiaSim aborts the whole
    cascade at parse time ("mismatch in the number of disjunctions of source segs
    and disjunctions of dest segs"), so no word could be simulated.
    FIXED: {a;e} > {∅;∅} / __ #   (both rules).
No other parse errors, runtime errors or undefined output symbols occurred.

B. STAGES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B1. 2.1-2.3 (interdentals > alveolars, s > š, ts/dz > s/z) sat BEFORE
    =Proto-Canaanite.
    Suchard (p.13) says these "are probably not Proto-Canaanite developments per
    se, but later, areal changes, affecting the Iron Age Canaanite languages".
    He adds that they "do not interact with the vocalic changes". Placed there, the
    Proto-Canaanite stage showed the post-merger consonant system.
    FIXED: moved, unchanged, to just after =Proto-Canaanite (before 3.1). Nothing in
    Ch.2 refers to the merged consonants, and the consonant lists of 3.1/3.8
    already assume the merged inventory.
The other stage boundaries follow Suchard's chapters:
  PNWS input > Proto-Canaanite > Proto-Hebrew > Proto-Jewish Hebrew > Masoretic.
The two cross-chapter moves are both stated by Suchard and are correct:
  - 5.1 into Proto-Hebrew ("Already at the Proto-Hebrew stage..."), before 3.1;
  - 3.9 before 3.4.

D. NOTES ADDED (rule lines unchanged; each needs the user's decision)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D1. 3.6a vs 3.7/3.8.
    The pretonic reduction of e/o (not before a) runs unconditionally, since the
    "analogical restoration before morpheme boundaries" is not modelled. It
    therefore removes the e/o that Suchard's own 3.7/3.8 examples need: ˁamoqīḿ >
    ˁamoqqīḿ comes out as *ˁaməqīm (tested), and kōhenīḿ as *kōhənīm. Only e/o
    before a stressed a/ā ever reach 3.7/3.8.
    Possible fix: if the lexicon marked suffix boundaries with "+", "no reduction
    before C+" would encode the restoration.
D2. 5.1.1b (ā > ā̊) placement.
    Suchard gives no date. As placed (first in Ch.5), the compensatory ā of 5.4
    stays [aː] (tested: the article before a geminate guttural comes out
    haːʔɔːdɔ́ːm, where Tiberian has qameṣ). Placing it later would instead back the
    lengthened vowel of guttural segolates (5.5 náˁr > nāˁar; Tiberian pataḥ).
    Neither order fits both.
D3. Exercise 3's unplaced changes (h-elision before a final short vowel; final
    stressed -ī > -ē).
    The extractor rightly flagged these rather than guessing. They are regular
    sound changes for Suchard, so they should be encoded once placed. I sharpened
    the conflict: the elision must follow 2.5 (raˀsahu > rōšō keeps the stress on
    the suffix), but must act on a final u, i.e. precede 2.4's word-final u > e.
    Both cannot hold unless 2.4 (hedged by Suchard: "seems to have") is taken not
    to apply to the suffix -hu.
D4. 3.2.1 feminine -āt > -ā is listed as "morphologically restricted", but
    Suchard's explanation is prosodic (t lost in prosodic word-final position,
    kept in the construct -at). If -āt# occurs only in that suffix at this stage,
    it could be encoded as t > ∅ / aː __ #.
D5. 5.1.5a (maqṭal- dissimilation) is encoded with a "#m a C C V" proxy. That hits
    any ma-CCV word, and it is inconsistent with the file's own comment-only
    treatment of the other pattern-restricted "minor sound changes" (2.2.3, 3.2.2).
    Kept, as the extractor marked it tentative.
